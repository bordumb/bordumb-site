There is a dangerous ambiguity in the phrase “formalizing an open problem.” It
can mean precisely stating the problem in a proof assistant. It can mean
building useful mathematical substrate around it. Or, heard carelessly, it can
sound like the problem has been solved.

The [Navier–Stokes proof map](https://github.com/bordumb/navier_stokes) is
explicit about the distinction:

> It is a Lean 4 and Mathlib project pointed at the Clay existence and
> smoothness problem—not to solve it, but to build a machine-checked map of what
> a proof or disproof would require.

The map is the deliverable.

## State the root without weakening it

For velocity $u$, pressure $p$, viscosity $\nu > 0$, and divergence-free
initial data $u_0$, the incompressible equations are:

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
+ (u \cdot \nabla)u
= \nu \Delta u - \nabla p + f,
\qquad
\nabla \cdot u = 0,
\qquad
u(\cdot,0)=u_0.
$$

A formal map begins by pinning the apex to the intended Clay statements. It
must not quietly replace the root with a theorem that merely has a similar
name—or a more convenient proposition.

In the repository, probes elaborate a statement pin and inspect the theorem's
axioms. A proof containing `sorry` exposes `sorryAx` and remains RED.

## Red is information

An open node is not a failed project state. It is a coordinate on the map.

The proved 1D model results are genuine, kernel-checked mathematics. They
validate Fourier bridges, identities, blowup mechanisms, and the checking
harness. They do not become progress on the 3D Clay apex merely because they
live in the same repository.

<Callout label="Status discipline">
  The exact counts will change; the distinction will not. Model nodes may close
  under kernel-clean probes while Clay-side nodes remain explicitly open. The
  former says nothing by itself about the latter.
</Callout>

## Why the traps matter

The apex probes retain several classes of counterexample:

- a genuine statement “proved” with `sorry`, which must remain RED;
- a mangled proposition with a convenient trivial proof, which the statement
  pin must reject;
- a tampered vendored root, which the integrity check must catch before
  elaboration.

This is more than test coverage. Each trap attacks a different way a formal
project can look complete while changing the meaning of completion.

If $P$ is the intended proposition, a trustworthy green verdict must establish
more than the existence of some term called `fefferman_D`:

$$
\operatorname{Green}(d)
\Rightarrow
\bigl(\operatorname{type}(d)=P\bigr)
\land
\bigl(\operatorname{axioms}(d)\subseteq W\bigr)
\land
\operatorname{rootIntegrity}.
$$

$W$ is the explicit axiom whitelist. The proposition, proof dependencies, and
root must all stay fixed.

## Formalization as intellectual honesty

A dependency map is useful because difficult proofs fail locally. One can see
which bridge is missing, which estimate is too weak, which formal vocabulary
does not yet exist, and which apparent route is blocked by a known no-go
result.

But the map has another value. It resists a very human pressure: the desire to
turn proximity, effort, or sophistication into completion.

A machine-checked red node says, with unusual calm: *not yet*. Preserving that
sentence may be the most important theorem the system knows.
